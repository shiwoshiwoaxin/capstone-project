I. Abstract

Research Overview
研究概述

This study systematically examines the formation mechanism of Hong Kong's multicultural landscape from the 1880s to the 1920s, a period defined by intensified Chinese migration across Southeast Asia and the consolidation of diasporic networks in the colony. It challenges both the "cultural desert" thesis and colonial-top-down narratives by foregrounding the agency of Southeast Asian Chinese networks—comprising native-place associations, kinship ties, and trade guilds—in constructing the city's cultural spaces from the bottom up.

Drawing on Lefebvre's theory of the production of space and integrating Historical GIS (HGIS), Social Network Analysis (SNA), three-dimensional digital reconstruction, and digital narrative, the research maps temples, native-place halls, theatres, and markets as nodes where geography, clanship, and industry networks intersected. By spatialising these network relations, it reveals how diasporic Chinese communities institutionalised cultural practices—charity ethics, theatrical inheritance, and commercial habits—in specific urban spaces, thereby weaving the very fabric of Hong Kong's cultural heritage.

II. Research Questions

Core Research Questions
核心研究问题

01

离散社群如何通过社会网络在特定空间制度化其文化实践？

How can diaspora communities institutionalise their cultural practices in specific spaces through social networks—such as the establishment of native-place halls and industry temples—and form places with cultural identity significance?

02

关键文化网络如何在空间中运作并推动文化模式的生成与扩散？

How can key cultural networks such as the Tung Wah Hospital, Pak Hok Tong, and Nam Pak Hong operate spatially and promote the generation and diffusion of specific cultural models—charity ethics, theatrical inheritance, and commercial habits?

03

不同社群构建的文化空间如何叠加并形成香港文化遗产？

How does the cultural space built by different communities overlap, functionally mix, and accumulate meaning over time, ultimately forming Hong Kong's cultural heritage?

III. Methodology

Research Methodology
研究方法

🗺️

历史GIS数据库建构

Historical GIS Database Construction

Data sources include Hong Kong Historical Archives, Hong Kong Memory Programme, old maps, newspaper advertisements, and community archives. Using ArcGIS Pro as the platform, temples, theatres, restaurants, and other cultural places are mapped individually with attributes for time, community attribution, and functional type.

ArcGIS Pro Geocoding Temporal Layers
🕸️

社会网络分析

Social Network Analysis

Using Gephi to analyse native-place, partnership, and in-law relationships among members of the Tung Wah Hospital board, the Pak Hok Tong committee, and the Nam Pak Hong firms. Combining spatial data to calculate network centrality and spatial clustering, identifying cultural hub figures and places.

Gephi Centrality Network Visualisation
🏛️

三维数字重建与空间分析

3D Digital Reconstruction & Spatial Analysis

Three typical spaces are selected for refined modelling: the Man Mo Temple (mid-19th century), the Taiping Theatre (1930s), and the Kowloon City Chiu Chow Daa Laang concentration area (1960s). Tools: Tinkercad for modelling, Agisoft Metashape for photogrammetry, and Unity for interactive scenes.

Tinkercad Metashape Unity
📊

数字叙事与可视化输出

Digital Narrative & Visualization

Using QGIS to integrate maps, network diagrams, 3D models, and historical texts. Three narrative threads: temples as social network nodes (arbitration → cultural landmarks); theatre network changes (Pak Hok Tong → Shaw Studios); dining space and identity (Chiu Chow Daa Laang → Kowloon market life).

QGIS Interactive Narrative Spatio-temporal Mapping
IV. Case Studies

Three Typical Spatial Cases

文武庙

Man Mo Temple

Reconstructing its indoor and outdoor layout based on records from the Hong Kong Chinese Daily and old photographs. As a centre of arbitration and belief, the temple demonstrates how Chinese communities constructed social authority and collective identity through religious space.

Arbitration Space • Belief Centre
1930s
🎭

太平戏院

Taiping Theatre

Combining architectural drawings and performance posters to restore its stage, seating, and backstage space. Analysing the performance relationship and community gathering mode reveals how entertainment spaces served as hubs for cultural transmission and social interaction.

Entertainment Geography • Community Hub
1960s
🍜

九龙城潮州打冷集中区

Kowloon City Chiu Chow Daa Laang

Based on oral histories and municipal archives, reconstructing the street shop layout and dining space usage. Analysing the relationship between dietary space and identity, showing how diasporic communities maintained group belonging through taste memory and everyday practice.

Dining Space • Identity Formation

VII. Literature Review

Literature Review
文献综述

I

华人离散研究与跨国网络

Overseas Chinese Research & Transnational Networks

From diaspora to spatial dynamics: Sinn (2013) reveals Hong Kong as a trans-Pacific hub; Carroll (2007) examines Chinese elite consultative strategies. Existing research lacks systematic spatialisation and visualisation of how ordinary overseas Chinese collectively shaped urban material space through daily practice.

II

殖民港城史与空间生产

Colonial Port City History & Social Production of Space

Challenging top-down narratives: Lefebvre (1991) emphasises space as product and medium of social relations. Chu (2021) analyses colonial land policies shaping urban geography, yet often regards residents as passive recipients. This study foregrounds how Chinese communities used social and cultural networks to create distinctive spaces.

III

数字人文转向与历史GIS

Digital Humanities Shift & Historical GIS

Gregory, Geddes, and Bodenhamer advocate spatial humanities using GIS to process historical materials. Bol (2015) notes GIS enables historians to "ask new questions and present evidence in a new way." This research innovates by deeply integrating SNA with Historical GIS—associating network nodes with precise coordinates to generate dynamic composite maps.

IX. Innovation

Research Innovation
研究创新

Innovation 01

自下而上的空间生产实证

Addressing the lack of systematic, visual empirical research on how ordinary overseas Chinese "bottom-up" shaped urban space, this study reveals the spatial practice logic of diaspora communities through temporal-spatial evolution and network correlation of cultural sites.

Innovation 02

HGIS与SNA的深度整合

Breaking through the limitations of static distribution maps and the separation of SNA from spatial contexts, this research associates network nodes with precise geographical coordinates, introduces centrality indicators, and generates dynamic composite maps that reveal the dual position of key figures and institutions in both social relations and geographic space.

Innovation 03

“空间—网络”方法重写城市史

Rewriting urban history from the perspective of overseas Chinese as active co-producers of urban space. This spatio-network method makes an exploratory contribution to digital humanities methodology, responding to Beale & Reilly's (2017) call for deeper integration of digital technology and humanistic inquiry.
